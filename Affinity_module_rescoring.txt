# Boltz-2 Affinity Module: Mechanistic Ablation and Target-Specific LoRA Finetuning

**A Formal Research Proposal**

---

## 1. Introduction

Recent structure-prediction foundation models such as Boltz-2 combine an
AlphaFold-style structural trunk with a dedicated *affinity head* that regresses
a continuous binding affinity ($\log_{10} \text{IC50}_{\mu\text{M}}$) and a
binary binder probability from a jointly predicted protein–ligand complex.
Because the affinity head is trained on very broad public data (ChEMBL, PDBBind,
etc.), it inherits two problems that limit its utility as a *virtual screening
rescorer*:

1. **Mechanistic opacity.** It is unclear which information channels feeding the
   affinity head (the pairformer trunk representation $z$, the single-token
   embeddings $s_{\text{inputs}}$, the embedded distogram, atom-level geometry,
   MSA profiles, and residue-type one-hots) actually drive the prediction, and
   how sensitive that prediction is to pose noise. Without this understanding
   any downstream deployment — including pose-quality filtering, saliency-based
   interpretation, or targeted finetuning — is guesswork.
2. **Generalization gap on individual targets.** The affinity head is a
   generalist. On a specific therapeutic target of interest, well-curated
   assay-consistent data from ChEMBL can outperform the pre-trained head by
   substantial margins — but only if adaptation is done in a
   parameter-efficient, capacity-comparable way that does not overwrite the
   trunk's structural knowledge.

This proposal describes two complementary experimental campaigns already
scaffolded in this repository — an **ablation study**
([ablation_experiment/](ablation_experiment)) and a **LoRA/full-finetune
comparison** ([fineturning_experiment/](fineturning_experiment)) — that
together answer *what the affinity module uses* and *how to specialize it*.
Both campaigns operate on physically realistic pose ensembles (DOCK3.8,
DiffDock, and Boltz-generated complexes) and are evaluated with early-recognition
metrics appropriate for virtual screening.

---

## 2. Aims

### Aim 1 — Mechanistic dissection of the Boltz-2 affinity head.
Quantify the individual and joint contributions of the affinity head's input
channels and sub-components, and its robustness to pose perturbations.

- **1a.** Rank the three top-level information channels (`distogram`, `z_trunk`,
  `s_inputs`) by necessity (single-channel ablation) and sufficiency
  (single-channel isolation with bias-only control).
- **1b.** Within `s_inputs`, decompose the contributions of the atom encoder
  (3D geometry, elements, vdW), the MSA profile, and the residue-type one-hot.
- **1c.** Characterize the spatial locality of the distogram signal via five
  masking patterns (whole, cross, ligand-involving, receptor–receptor,
  distance-cutoff).
- **1d.** Measure robustness under Gaussian coordinate noise applied to (i) the
  ligand only, (ii) the receptor only, and (iii) all atoms, across
  $\sigma \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0\}$ Å with seed replicates.
- **1e.** Localize per-residue importance via a *residue leave-one-out (LOO)*
  saliency analysis on the pair representation, yielding per-target hotspot
  fingerprints comparable to experimental mutagenesis.

### Aim 2 — Target-specific parameter-efficient finetuning.
Determine whether a small LoRA adapter can match a full finetune of the affinity
sub-tree on curated, assay-grouped ChEMBL data for two clinically relevant
aminergic GPCRs (DRD4, 5-HT2A).

- **2a.** Curate assay-consistent ChEMBL bioactivity data for DRD4 (CHEMBL219)
  and 5-HT2A (CHEMBL224) with censoring flags and assay-group identifiers.
- **2b.** Generate Boltz-2-predicted holoprotein poses for every ligand
  (no external docking required), so training and inference share the same
  featurization distribution.
- **2c.** Train **LoRA adapters** (`heads_pairformer` target spec, rank 8/32)
  and **matched full finetunes** of the same sub-tree, with identical data,
  loss, and early stopping — enabling a strict parameter-efficiency comparison.
- **2d.** Explore hyperparameter sensitivity, including a Huber-$\delta$ scan
  and a rank / batch-size / dataset-size scaling study
  ([huber_delta_scan/](fineturning_experiment/huber_delta_scan),
  [scaling/](fineturning_experiment/scaling)).
- **2e.** Validate adapted models against held-out in-vitro data
  ([validation_data/](fineturning_experiment/validation_data)) that was not
  seen during ChEMBL curation.

### Aim 3 — Integrated evaluation as a virtual screening rescorer.
Compare vanilla Boltz-2, LoRA-adapted Boltz-2, full-finetuned Boltz-2, and
external baselines (DOCK3.8, DiffDock, "OG" Boltz-1 affinity) on the DUD-E /
DUD-Z benchmark using bootstrap-CI log-AUC as the primary metric, and connect
the ablation results (Aim 1) to observed screening behavior (Aim 3).

## 3. Design and Methodology

### 3.1 Model under study

All experiments target the **Boltz-2 affinity head** (`boltz==2.2.1`). The head
consumes three streams produced by the frozen structural trunk:

| Stream | Origin | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `distogram` | Boltz trunk | Embedded protein–ligand pairwise distance matrix |
| `z_trunk` | Pairformer | Pair representation (MSA, templates, relative position, bond info) |
| `s_inputs` | InputEmbedder | Per-token single representation composed of `atom_encoder` + `msa_profile` + `res_type` |

The trunk is expensive (~10 s per complex); the head is cheap (~10 ms).
The ablation pipeline exploits this asymmetry by caching trunk outputs once per
`(receptor, ligand)` pair and replaying the head under many masking
configurations.

### 3.2 Ablation matrix (Aim 1)

Implemented in
[run_feature_ablation.py](ablation_experiment/experiment_scripts/run_feature_ablation.py),
[run_feature_ablation_dudez.py](ablation_experiment/experiment_scripts/run_feature_ablation_dudez.py),
[run_distogram_ablation.py](ablation_experiment/experiment_scripts/run_distogram_ablation.py),
[run_residue_loo.py](ablation_experiment/experiment_scripts/run_residue_loo.py),
[queue_boltz_rescoring.py](ablation_experiment/experiment_scripts/queue_boltz_rescoring.py).

**Channel ablations.** Multiplicative masking zeros a stream and re-runs the
head. Configurations: `baseline`, `no_distogram`, `no_z_trunk`, `no_s_inputs`
(necessity); `distogram_only`, `z_trunk_only`, `s_inputs_only`, `bias_only`
(sufficiency).

**Sub-component ablations.** Analogous necessity / sufficiency sweeps over
`atom_encoder`, `msa_profile`, `res_type` within `s_inputs`.

**Distogram spatial masks.** Five patterns — `none`, `zero_all`, `zero_cross`
(protein↔ligand only), `zero_ligand`, `zero_receptor`, `distance_cutoff` (zero
pairs beyond $d$ Å, default 8 Å).

**Pose-noise perturbations.** Gaussian noise
$\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_3)$ is applied in coordinate space before
featurization for
$\sigma \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0\}$ Å (ligand-only) and
$\sigma \in \{1.0, 5.0\}$ Å (receptor-only and all-atom controls),
each with seed replicates `{101, 202, 303}`. This yields ~48 named
configurations in total.

**Residue LOO saliency.** For each receptor token within `--radius` Å (default
8) of any ligand atom, the corresponding row and column of $z$ are zeroed and
the head is re-run. The per-residue signal is
$\Delta = \text{baseline pIC50} - \text{LOO pIC50}$. Outputs are long-form CSVs
consumed by
[plot_residue_saliency.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/plot_residue_saliency.py)
to produce per-target hotspot heatmaps and top-10 rankings.

**Launcher infrastructure.**
[queue_boltz_rescoring.py](ablation_experiment/experiment_scripts/queue_boltz_rescoring.py)
submits per-receptor jobs with configurable `--max-concurrent` and dependency
chaining; the DUDEZ array wrapper
[submit_feature_ablation_dudez.sh](ablation_experiment/experiment_scripts/submit_feature_ablation_dudez.sh)
discovers Boltz-predicted receptors and forwards experiment flags.

### 3.3 Finetuning pipeline (Aim 2)

End-to-end pipeline in
[fineturning_experiment/run_pipeline.sh](fineturning_experiment/run_pipeline.sh),
driven by [config.env](fineturning_experiment/config.env) and staged as
sequential SLURM jobs:

1. **`01_pull_chembl.slurm`** — [pull_chembl_affinity_data.py](scripts/pull_chembl_affinity_data.py)
   queries ChEMBL REST for CHEMBL219 / CHEMBL224, filters on standard type
   (Ki/Kd/IC50/EC50/AC50/XC50), applies confidence cutoffs (B ≥ 7, F ≥ 6),
   canonicalizes SMILES via `boltz.data.parse.smiles_standardize`, and emits
   `data/chembl_D4_5HT2A_curated.csv` with `log10_aff_uM`, `assay_chembl_id`,
   `is_censored`, and `n_measurements`.
2. **`02_precompute_msas.slurm`** — [precompute_msas.py](fineturning_experiment/precompute_msas.py)
   makes exactly two ColabFold MMseqs2 calls (one per receptor) and caches
   `.a3m` under the canonical sequence hash. All later stages read from this
   cache via `$BOLTZ_MSA_CACHE_DIR`; the MSA server is otherwise disabled by
   repo policy.
3. **`03_predict_poses.slurm`** — [prepare_predict_inputs.py](fineturning_experiment/prepare_predict_inputs.py)
   emits per-ligand Boltz YAMLs (`PREDICT_RECYCLING_STEPS=3`,
   `PREDICT_SAMPLING_STEPS=200`, `PREDICT_DIFFUSION_SAMPLES=1`), `boltz predict`
   generates rank-0 complexes, and [collect_predicted_poses.py](fineturning_experiment/collect_predicted_poses.py)
   flattens results into `poses/{DRD4,5HT2A}/{molecule_chembl_id}.pdb`. **This
   means training and inference see poses drawn from the same distribution.**
4. **`04{a,b}_prepare_manifest_*.slurm`** — [build_lora_manifest.py](fineturning_experiment/build_lora_manifest.py)
   joins curated CSV with existing pose files, drops censored rows, and emits
   `manifests/lora_manifest_{DRD4,5HT2A}.csv` with columns
   `name, ligand, receptor, target, structure, group_id, is_binder`.
5. **`05{a,b}_train_*.slurm`** — `boltz lora train` with
   `rank=8, alpha=16, lr=1e-4, batch_size=32, epochs=20,
   loss=censored_boltz2_affinity, target_spec=heads_pairformer`. Adapters land
   in `adapters/{drd4,5ht2a}_..._rank_8_censored_data/` and are registered in
   [adapters/registry.json](fineturning_experiment/adapters).
6. **`05{c,d}_finetune_*.slurm`** — Matched *full-finetune control* with the
   same data, loss, and target sub-tree, but $10\times$ lower learning rate
   ($1\text{e-}5$) to stabilize full-parameter updates. Outputs to
   `finetunes/{drd4,5ht2a}_full_v1/`.

**LoRA parameterization.** For every adapted `nn.Linear`:
$$y = W_0 x + \tfrac{\alpha}{r}\, B A\, x, \qquad A \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{\text{in}}},\ B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}\times r},\ B \equiv 0 \text{ at init.}$$
With `heads_pairformer` and rank 8, this covers ~102 layers (~1–5% of the
affinity sub-tree); a v2 registry entry uses rank 32 (~204 layers).

**Loss.** `censored_boltz2_affinity` combines (i) intra-assay Huber on
$\log_{10}\text{IC50}_{\mu\text{M}}$, (ii) intra-assay pairwise Huber on
$\hat{y}_i - \hat{y}_j$ (which cancels the Cheng–Prusoff offset and legitimizes
mixing Ki/Kd/IC50), and (iii) an optional BCE branch on `is_binder`
(threshold $\log_{10}\text{IC50}_{\mu\text{M}} \le 1.0$, i.e. IC50 ≤ 10 µM,
matching the Boltz-2 paper convention). An `AssayGroupedSampler` guarantees
$\ge 4$ compounds per assay per batch so the pairwise term is always active.

**Ancillary studies.**
[huber_delta_scan/](fineturning_experiment/huber_delta_scan) systematically
varies $\delta$ (MSE↔MAE transition), and
[scaling/](fineturning_experiment/scaling) sweeps rank, batch size, and
dataset size. `register_v2_adapters.py`, `recover_adapter.py`, and
`recover_5ht2a_adapter.py` handle registry migrations and mid-run recovery.

### 3.4 Evaluation methodology (Aim 3)

Implemented in [ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts):

- **log-AUC (early recognition).** [compute_logauc_bootstrap.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/compute_logauc_bootstrap.py)
  computes the log-scaled area under the TPR curve between $10^{-3}$ and $1$
  FPR (10 000 bins, linear interpolation), scaled to $[0, 100]$; random ≈ 28–35.
  Bootstrap: 100 replicates stratified per `(experiment, receptor)`; actives
  and decoys are resampled independently with replacement, then aggregated
  across receptors. Random labels: actives = non-ZINC IDs, decoys = ZINC IDs.
- **Distribution statistics.** [compute_distribution_stats.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/compute_distribution_stats.py)
  reports per `(experiment, receptor)` mean/std pIC50 for actives and decoys,
  Cohen's $d$, and rank-based AUROC (lower-is-better aware); per-experiment
  cross-receptor summaries in `distribution_stats_summary.csv`.
- **Score aggregation.** [combine_scores.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/combine_scores.py)
  outer-joins DOCK3.8, "OG" Boltz-1 affinity, and Boltz-2 rescoring outputs
  into [analysis_data/combined_scores.csv](ablation_experiment/analysis_data/combined_scores.csv).
- **Visualization.** [plot_ridgeline.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/plot_ridgeline.py)
  (KDE ridges of log-AUC per method), [plot_scatter.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/plot_scatter.py)
  (Boltz vs. DOCK3.8 / OG with Pearson/Spearman + OLS), [plot_residue_saliency.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/plot_residue_saliency.py)
  (per-target hotspot heatmaps).
- **TL;DR summarizer.** [bootstrap_tldr.py](ablation_experiment/analysis_scripts/bootstrap_tldr.py)
  produces a compact ranked table of experiments with mean log-AUC and 95% CI.

## 4. Datasets and Evaluation

### 4.1 Ablation and screening benchmark (Aims 1 & 3)

- **Receptors.** 43 DUD-E / DUD-Z targets in [ablation_experiment/receptors/](ablation_experiment/receptors)
  spanning kinases (ABL1, EGFR, KIT, LCK, MK01, SRC, ROCK1, PLK1, FGFR1, MAPK2,
  CSF1R), GPCRs (AA2AR, ADRB2, CXCR4, DRD4), nuclear receptors (ANDR, PPARA),
  proteases (HIVPR, FA10, FA7, RENI, THRB, TRY1, UROK), and diverse enzymes.
- **Pose sources.** DOCK3.8 multi-pose MOL2 in
  [DOCK3.8_poses/](ablation_experiment/DOCK3.8_poses), DiffDock scores in
  [analysis_data/diffdock_scores/](ablation_experiment/analysis_data/diffdock_scores),
  and Boltz-predicted complexes for the DUDEZ variant.
- **Ligand labels.** Convention shared by all scripts: **actives = ChEMBL IDs
  (non-ZINC prefix)**, **decoys = `ZINC` IDs**.
- **External baselines.** [dock3.8_scores/](ablation_experiment/analysis_data/dock3.8_scores),
  [OG_affinity_scores/master_scores.csv](ablation_experiment/analysis_data/OG_affinity_scores),
  and the DiffDock TSVs.

### 4.2 ChEMBL finetuning datasets (Aim 2)

- **Targets.** DRD4 (CHEMBL219) and 5-HT2A (CHEMBL224) — related aminergic
  GPCRs with abundant published binding data and clinical relevance
  (antipsychotics, psychedelics).
- **Curation.** See §3.3 stage 01. Filters keep only high-confidence
  biochemical/functional assays with standard IC50/Ki/Kd/EC50/AC50/XC50 values,
  RDKit-canonicalized and Boltz-standardized SMILES, and explicit censoring
  flags. Assay groups are preserved throughout for the pairwise loss term.
- **Held-out validation.** [validation_data/D4_in_vitro_data.csv](fineturning_experiment/validation_data/D4_in_vitro_data.csv)
  and [validation_data/5ht2a_in_vitro_data.csv](fineturning_experiment/validation_data/5ht2a_in_vitro_data.csv)
  provide external in-vitro measurements not seen during ChEMBL curation.

### 4.3 Primary and secondary evaluation metrics

| Metric | Rationale | Where computed |
|---|---|---|
| **log-AUC (0.1%–100% FPR)** | Early recognition; standard for VS | `compute_logauc_bootstrap.py` |
| **Bootstrap 95% CI** on log-AUC | Statistical significance across receptors | same |
| **Cohen's $d$, AUROC** | Effect size, ranking quality | `compute_distribution_stats.py` |
| **Regression on continuous $\log_{10}\text{IC50}$** | Sanity check for Aim 2 (MAE, RMSE, Pearson $r$) | training logs |
| **Per-residue $|\Delta|$ saliency** | Mechanistic interpretation | `plot_residue_saliency.py` |
| **Robustness curves** (log-AUC vs. $\sigma$) | Pose-noise sensitivity | ablation CSV |

## 5. Expected Outcomes

**From Aim 1.**
1. The **distogram** channel is expected to be the largest single contributor
   to affinity accuracy; `zero_all` and `zero_cross` masks should collapse
   log-AUC to near-random, while `zero_receptor` should have small effect.
2. `z_trunk` and `s_inputs` are likely partially redundant: single-channel
   removal degrades performance modestly, but joint removal (bias-only) yields
   random-quality scores.
3. Within `s_inputs`, `atom_encoder` (3D geometry) is expected to dominate
   `msa_profile` and `res_type`; MSA profile still contributes non-trivially
   because it encodes evolutionary conservation of pocket residues.
4. Pose-noise robustness curves are expected to show a soft knee near
   $\sigma \approx 1$ Å for ligand-only noise (roughly the resolution of a good
   docking pose) with much steeper degradation for receptor / all-atom noise,
   consistent with a model that has learned distance-based interaction
   fingerprints.
5. Residue LOO should identify pharmacophorically meaningful residues
   (aminergic-binding aspartate in TM3 of GPCRs, hinge residues of kinases,
   catalytic dyads/triads of proteases) — providing a strong sanity check
   and a candidate tool for mutant-effect prediction.

**From Aim 2.**
6. **LoRA (rank 8) matches full finetune within noise** on DRD4 and 5-HT2A at
   a fraction of the parameters (~1–5%), particularly at moderate data sizes
   (hundreds of assay-consistent labels). The scaling study should reveal a
   crossover at which full FT begins to win, informing when LoRA is enough.
7. Both LoRA and full FT substantially outperform vanilla Boltz-2 on target-
   specific ranking (regression Pearson $r$ and held-out log-AUC), while
   preserving performance on unrelated DUD-E/Z receptors from the ablation
   benchmark (i.e. no catastrophic forgetting of the structural trunk, since
   only `heads_pairformer` is adapted).
8. The Huber-$\delta$ scan should identify a robust plateau (e.g. $\delta
   \in [1, 3]$) with the pairwise term making training insensitive to the
   absolute assay offset.

**From Aim 3.**
9. A joint picture: the *sub-components* the ablations identify as important
   should be exactly the ones whose adaptation via LoRA yields the largest
   Aim-2 gains, closing the mechanistic-to-practical loop.

## 6. Pitfalls and Mitigations

| Pitfall | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Decoy/active label leakage via ID conventions** (ZINC vs ChEMBL) | Inflated log-AUC | Documented and enforced by all analysis scripts; audit `combined_scores.csv` on load |
| **MSA server disabled but pipeline needs MSAs** | Silent fallback / crashes | Repo policy: `precompute_msas.py` runs once per target; canonical hash + legacy symlinks; `$BOLTZ_MSA_CACHE_DIR` honored everywhere |
| **`GLIBCXX_3.4.34` mismatch** on Great Lakes after `module load cuda/...` | Boltz fails to import | All `.slurm` scripts prepend `${CONDA_PREFIX}/lib` to `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` |
| **Trunk cost dominates ablation matrix** | 48 configs × trunk = infeasible | Cache trunk once per `(receptor, ligand)`; replay head with masks |
| **Full-finetune divergence** relative to LoRA at matched LR | Unfair capacity comparison | Full FT uses $10\times$ lower LR (1e-5), gradient clip 1.0, weight decay 1e-4, early stopping (patience 5, min-delta 0.005) |
| **Batch size < 4** breaks pairwise loss | Loss term silently zero | `AssayGroupedSampler` enforces ≥ 4 compounds/assay per batch |
| **Censored measurements skew regression** | Biased MAE | Dropped by default; retained via `--keep-censored` for censored-aware loss experiments |
| **Bootstrap under-samples rare actives** | Wide CI | Stratified per-receptor bootstrap with 100 replicates; report CI, not just point estimates |
| **Boltz-predicted poses are self-generated** (Aim 2) | Train/test distribution match too tight | Held-out validation on in-vitro data + external DUD-Z evaluation with DOCK3.8 poses breaks any circularity |
| **Adapter registry drift** across renames | Broken checkpoints | `register_v2_adapters.py` / `recover_*_adapter.py` reconcile registry; `adapters/registry.json` is the source of truth |

## 7. Anticipated Challenges

1. **Compute budget.** `03_predict_poses.slurm` is the tall pole (up to 6 days
   on an H200 for a few thousand ligands with 3 recycling / 200 sampling
   steps). Mitigations: reduce `PREDICT_DIFFUSION_SAMPLES` to 1 (already done),
   and reserve high-quality prediction for the finetuning targets only.
2. **Assay heterogeneity in ChEMBL.** Even after confidence filtering, IC50
   values across labs vary by 0.5–1.0 log units. The intra-assay pairwise Huber
   loss is the principal mitigation; the Huber-$\delta$ scan quantifies
   residual sensitivity.
3. **Interpreting isolation vs. necessity.** A channel can be *necessary*
   (removing it hurts) without being *sufficient* (isolating it does not
   recover baseline) because the head is trained on the joint distribution.
   The `bias_only` control anchors the sufficiency scale.
4. **Statistical power on 43 receptors.** Log-AUC variance across DUD-E/Z
   targets is high. Bootstrap CIs prevent over-claiming; per-target reporting
   is preserved in `distribution_stats_per_receptor.csv`.
5. **Distinguishing genuine improvement from memorization** in Aim 2. Held-out
   in-vitro validation sets and, ideally, temporal splits (post-cutoff ChEMBL
   releases) are required. The current pipeline supports the first; the second
   is a straightforward extension of `pull_chembl_affinity_data.py`.
6. **Extrapolating LOO residue saliency to causal claims.** LOO gives a
   *model-attribution* signal, not an experimental one. Cross-reference with
   known mutagenesis data (e.g. GPCRdb) before interpreting hotspots as
   drug-design guidance.
7. **Reproducibility across environments.** Trunk stochasticity (diffusion
   sampling) means exact score reproduction requires fixed seeds; the pose
   noise experiments already report per-seed variability, and full-run
   reproducibility is guarded by pinned versions in
   [boltz_affinity.yaml](boltz_affinity.yaml).

## 9. Summary

The two experimental campaigns already scaffolded in this repository jointly
answer a central question about protein–ligand foundation models:
*which parts of the affinity module carry the signal, and can the module be
cheaply specialized to a target of interest without disturbing the trunk?*
The ablation campaign gives a mechanistic map (channels, sub-components, pose
noise, per-residue attribution); the LoRA / full-finetune campaign gives a
practical protocol grounded in a rigorous, capacity-matched control. Together
they define a research program that connects interpretation to deployment for
Boltz-2 as a virtual-screening rescorer.
